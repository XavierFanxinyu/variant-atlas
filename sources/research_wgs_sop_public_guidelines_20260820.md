# WGS SOP public-guidance crosswalk research

Lookup date: 2026-08-20 (Asia/Shanghai)

Purpose: identify public, primary guidance suitable for a public WES/WGS interpretation learning site. The internal SOP is used only to identify workflow topics; proprietary thresholds, system operations, screenshots, templates, and example wording are not reproduced.

## Primary sources

1. Rehder C, et al. *Next-generation sequencing for constitutional variants in the clinical laboratory, 2021 revision: a technical standard of the ACMG.* Genetics in Medicine. 2021. PMID 33927380. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33927380/
   - Public anchor for assay validation, quality monitoring, interpretation, reporting, and method limitations across panels, exomes, and genomes.

2. Riggs ER, et al. *Technical standards for the interpretation and reporting of constitutional copy-number variants: a joint consensus recommendation of ACMG and ClinGen.* Genetics in Medicine. 2020. PMID 31690835. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31690835/
   - Public anchor for loss/gain CNV scoring, five-tier classification, and separating variant classification from patient-specific significance.

3. Raca G, et al. *Points to consider in the detection of germline structural variants using next-generation sequencing: an ACMG statement.* Genetics in Medicine. 2023. PMID 36507974. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36507974/
   - Public anchor for germline SV detection limits, balanced rearrangements, CNVs, and ES/GS-specific considerations.

4. McCormick EM, et al. *Specifications of the ACMG/AMP standards and guidelines for mitochondrial DNA variant interpretation.* Human Mutation. 2020. PMID 32906214. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32906214/
   - Public anchor for mtDNA-specific interpretation, including heteroplasmy, maternal inheritance, threshold effects, haplogroups, and mitochondrial databases.

5. Seifert BA, et al. *Myotonic dystrophy type 1 testing, 2024 revision: an ACMG technical standard.* Genetics in Medicine. 2024. PMID 38836869. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38836869/
   - Example of disease-specific repeat-expansion standards; supports routing STR teaching to disorder-specific guidance rather than a single universal WGS rule.

6. Lee K, et al. *ACMG SF v3.3 list for reporting of secondary findings in clinical exome and genome sequencing.* Genetics in Medicine. 2025. PMID 40568962. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40568962/
   - Current secondary-findings list (v3.3; 84 genes) and versioning anchor.

7. ClinGen Secondary Findings Resource. https://search.clinicalgenome.org/kb/genes/acmgsf
   - Current reportability and curation portal for ACMG SF v3.3.

8. ClinGen Variant Classification Guidance. https://www.clinicalgenome.org/tools/clingen-variant-classification-guidance/
   - Current sequence-variant interpretation recommendations and VCEP routing.

9. Richards S, et al. *Standards and guidelines for the interpretation of sequence variants: ACMG/AMP joint consensus.* Genetics in Medicine. 2015. PMID 25741868. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25741868/
   - Base five-tier sequence-variant framework, applicable to exome and genome sequencing.

## Design conclusions

- Treat WGS as a multi-channel case workflow, not as “WES plus more genes.”
- Keep assay-specific QC and reportability thresholds configurable; do not publish a company-specific fixed cutoff as a universal standard.
- Use separate interpretation routes for sequence variants, CNV loss/gain, balanced/complex SV, mtDNA, repeat expansions, aneuploidy/polyploidy, and LOH/ROH.
- Do not teach a deterministic P/LP rule for balanced SV when the evidence base is mechanism- and breakpoint-dependent.
- Keep prenatal and postnatal return-of-results policies separate, with consent scope and current policy version recorded.
- Keep infectious-agent analysis outside the core germline-variant curriculum unless supported by a separately validated assay and clinical microbiology guidance.
